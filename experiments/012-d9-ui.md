## Question

Job: **verify, and try to break** (PM note `2026-10-06-exp-d9-ui.md`).

Claim 007 (approved) proves ucp convergence of the finite-grid fund W^(N) to the continuous wealth V^π when the price
stays positive. Its expectation limit (7) holds only if {W^(N)_T} is uniformly integrable, which the claim states as
an extra hypothesis. Math's investigation of whether UI holds when zero is inaccessible (`math/d9-ui-investigation`)
is not reviewed and is not used here.

This experiment measures, for driftless CEV dS = S^β dW with S_0 = 1 (zero inaccessible: I₀ = ∞ for β ≥ 1):
1. E W^(N)_T and E|W^(N)_T| against the continuous E V_T, as N grows;
2. the UI diagnostic sup_N E[|W^(N)_T| 1{|W^(N)_T| > K}], for K ∈ {2, 5, 10, 50}.

**What would count against UI.** The tail expectation for some K fails to fall toward 0 as K grows, across the N
grid; or it grows with N beyond Monte Carlo error. **What would count against the mean limit.** E W^(N)_T that does
not approach E V_T as N grows. Both are evidence about these cases only.

## Design

**Cases.**
- β ∈ {1, 1.5, 2}, π ∈ {−1, 1/4, 3/4, 2, 3}, T = 1.
- N ∈ {4, 16, 64, 252, 1008} rebalances per year.
- The fund follows claim 005's recursion (1), W_0 = 1, at zero rate and no cost.

**Exact simulation** on 4032 = lcm(64, 1008) steps per year, the lcm used in experiment 009. Every N is subsampled
from it, with common random numbers across N and π.
- β = 1: geometric Brownian motion, S = exp(W − t/2).
- β = 2: S = 1/|x_0 + B_t|, with B a three-dimensional Brownian motion and x_0 = (1, 0, 0).
- β = 1.5: S = 4/R_t², with R = |x_0 + B_t|, B a four-dimensional Brownian motion and x_0 = (2, 0, 0, 0). That is
  the exact Bessel(4) representation of R = S^{−1/2}/(β − 1).
- Paths: 400,000 per β, in batches. Seeds: `SeedSequence(20261012)`, spawned per β and per batch.

**Monte Carlo estimates,** with SEs (sample SD/√n):
- E W^(N)_T and E|W^(N)_T|;
- E[|W^(N)_T| 1{|W^(N)_T| > K}] for each K and N;
- the supremum over N of the latter.

The SEs assume finite variance. For the heavier cases this may fail. So, for each estimate, the share of the total
contributed by the largest 10 paths is also reported, as a heavy-tail flag.

**Targeted values.**
- Continuous E V_T: experiment 008's `continuous_defect` (experiment 006's method P, both endpoints, any π) at its
  k = 6 truncation and h = 0.005, with dt = 1/4032. For β = 1, E V_T = 1 exactly.
- Discrete E W^(N)_T: experiment 008's exact backward recursion `fund_defects`, with Δ = 1/N and m = 40.
  - It is run where that recursion is stable: π ∈ {1/4, 3/4} at every N, and the other π at N ∈ {4, 16} only.
    Experiment 008's Deviation 3 found the error amplified for π ∉ [0, 1] over many intervals.
  - It serves as an independent check of the Monte Carlo mean. Agreement means within 3 Monte Carlo SEs plus the
    recursion's own error estimate.

**Resources.** Run from inside `.worktrees/analyst`, on cores − 3 workers.

**Outputs.**
- `experiments/012/results.json` and `experiments/012/summary.csv`.
- Regenerate with `uv run python experiments/012/run.py`.

## Scope correction (2026-10-06, at math's request in `2026-10-06-exp012-variance-scope.md`)

The infinite-variance problem in reading 3 is not limited to π > 1. It covers **every β = 1.5 cell, at every finite
N, for every π ≠ 0**, including π = 1/4 and π = 3/4. Math's argument (FINDINGS, newest entry):
- Condition on the last rebalance. The next four-dimensional Gaussian vector has positive density at zero, so the
  radius density is about r³ near 0.
- With X_T² = 16/r⁴ this gives E[X_T² | F_prev] = ∞.
- Terminal wealth is affine in X_T with a nonzero holding, so it has infinite second moment. So do |W^(N)_T| and the
  truncated tail payoffs |W| 1{|W| > K}.

Consequences for this report:
- In all β = 1.5 rows, the "± SE" figures (sample SD/√n) are not valid error bars.
- The "3 SE plus recursion error" agreement criterion is not justified in those cells.
- A small top-ten share, as for β = 1.5 at π = 1/4 and 3/4, does not establish finite variance.

The descriptive estimates and the comparisons with the exact recursion stand as reported. The recursion values are
deterministic and do not depend on the Monte Carlo variance. For β = 1.5, read the agreements as descriptive closeness
of the point estimates, not as a test.

The β = 1 and β = 2 rows are not affected by the infinite-variance problem:
- For β = 1 (lognormal), all moments are finite. The β = 1 SEs are still not calibrated confidence intervals; see
  the next section.
- For β = 2, S = 1/R with R Bessel(3) has E S² < ∞.

This correction does not refute first-moment UI, and it changes no number in the tables. No new simulation was run.

## β = 1 analytic variance benchmark (2026-10-06, at math's request in `2026-10-06-exp012-lognormal-moments.md`)

For β = 1, the rebalancing intervals have independent Gaussian increments, so the moments of W^(N)_T are exact. At
T = 1:

  E W = 1,  E W² = [1 + π²(e^{1/N} − 1)]^N.

**For π = 3 and N = 1008** (math's figures; I re-derived the arithmetic):
- E W² = 7820.3550.
- So the true SD of a 400,000-path sample mean is √((E W² − 1)/400,000) = 0.1398155.
- The stored sample SE is 0.0332768. It captures 5.66% of the theoretical variance.

**What this means:**
- The variance is finite here, but the sample greatly underestimated it in this realization. The lognormal-type
  tail is heavy, and 400,000 paths rarely reach the paths that carry E W². This is a different problem from the
  infinite variance at β = 1.5.
- It does not invalidate the consistency of the sample variance, and it does not refute uniform integrability.
- The model-based "± SE" figures in the β = 1 rows are not calibrated confidence intervals. That matters most at large
  π and N, and the agreements there are descriptive.

`uv run python checks/007/check.py` independently computes the interval moments by Gaussian quadrature and prints all
25 β = 1 comparisons. The cross-moment formulas and the L² benchmark distances are in the newest
`board/FINDINGS.md` entry. No number in the tables changed, and no new simulation was run.

**Correction, 2026-10-06 (reviewer, on math's inbox note): infinite variance also for β = 1.5 at π = 1/4 and 3/4.** I checked math's argument independently, and it is right.
- **The argument.** Condition on the 4-dimensional Gaussian vector at the last rebalance. The terminal vector is Gaussian around it, with density positive at the origin. So R_T has density ≈ c·r³ near 0, and E[S_T² | F_{t_{N−1}}] = ∫16r⁻⁴·c·r³ dr = ∞ (logarithmically). Terminal wealth is W_{N−1}(1−π) + (πW_{N−1}/S_{N−1})·S_T, with a strictly positive holding for 0 < π ≤ 1. So E[W^(N)_T²] = ∞ for every finite N. The same holds for |W| and for the truncated tail payoffs |W|1{|W| > K}.
- **Numerical confirmation (N = 1, T = 1).** R_T² is noncentral χ² with 4 degrees of freedom and noncentrality 4. Quadrature of E[S_T²; S_T ≤ M] = ∫_{4/M} 16u⁻² f(u) du grows by exactly 1.2465 per decade of M at M ∈ [10⁴, 10⁸]. That is the predicted 4e⁻²·ln 10, so the divergence is logarithmic. For W at π = 1/4 and 3/4 the predicted per-decade growth is π² times that, 0.078 and 0.70. A 4·10⁶-path simulation shows 0.087 and 0.70 over the first decades, then no further growth only because the sample has no draws in the far tail. That is exactly why sample SDs understate the error here.
- **What this changes, in the experiment and in my review above.**
  - The β = 1.5 rows' "± SE", the experiment's 3-SE agreement test between Monte Carlo and the exact recursion in those cells, and my "within combined Monte Carlo error" for β = 1.5 are not valid finite-variance statements. They are descriptive comparisons. Because the divergence is only logarithmic, the stated SEs understate the true spread modestly, not catastrophically.
  - A small top-ten share does not cure this.
- **What it does not change.**
  - The means are finite and the estimators consistent. The exact recursion values are deterministic.
  - Both runs show E W climbing toward E V at π = 3/4, and the qualitative UI reading stands. Infinite second moments do not bear on first-moment uniform integrability.
  - β = 2 (E S^p < ∞ for p < 3) and β = 1 have finite variance, and their error bars stand.
- **The verdict stands:** the reported numbers reproduce. Only the inferential reading of the β = 1.5 error bars is corrected, here and by the analyst's own correction note.

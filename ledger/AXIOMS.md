# Ledger of cited results

Everything the project takes from the literature instead of proving. One entry per result.

A ledger entry needs no human approval: it is usable once its source text is in `refs/text/` and the
auditor has checked it against that text on the same branch, leaving a line in `AUDIT_LOG.md`.

Each entry carries:

- **Source:** bibliography key, the exact theorem/section, and where in `refs/text/` it is.
- **Statement (source notation):** quoted verbatim. Quote, do not paraphrase.
- **Statement (our notation):** the form the project uses, with every specialization named.
- **Assumptions in the source:** including any the project silently relies on.
- **Simplifications we impose:** each one, and why it does not strengthen the conclusion.
- **Formal:** how it enters the formalization — a hypothesis structure and its instance, or, for a
  closed algebraic proposition, the lemma that proves it. See rule 6.
- **Audit:** the `AUDIT_LOG.md` line that passed it.

not a real number, so the queue does not offer it for audit; copy it for the first entry.

### AX-01  Martingale criterion with both endpoints
Source: [mijatovic2010martingale], Theorem 2.1, its following remark and Corollary 2.2, printed p. 8; setup (4)–(8), (15)–(19), (24), (26), printed pp. 4–8; `refs/text/mijatovic2010martingale.md`.

> Theorem 2.1 Let the functions μ, σ , and b satisfy conditions (5), (6) and (8), and
> Y b eas o l u t i o no fS D E(4) that possibly exits the state space J = (l, r). Then the
> process Z given by (7) is a martingale if and only if at least one of the conditions
> (a)–(b) below is satisﬁed AND at least one of the conditions (c)–(d) below is satisﬁed:
> (a) ~Y does not exit J at r, i.e. (20)[ equivalently, (21)] is not satisﬁed;

> (c) ~Y does not exit J at l, i.e. (22)[ equivalently, (23)] is not satisﬁed;
> (d) l is good, i.e. (26)[ equivalently, (27)] is satisﬁed.
> Remark The same condition is necessary and sufﬁcient for Z to be a martingale on
> the time interval [0, T] for any ﬁxed T ∈ (0,∞) (see the proof of Theorem 2.1 in
> Sect. 6).

> Corollary 2.2 Assume that Y does not exit its state space and let the assumptions of
> Theorem 2.1 be satisﬁed. Then Z is a martingale if and only if~Y does not exit its state
> space.

Statement (our notation): For a diffusion S on J = (l,r), started at s₀ ∈ J under Q, with drift μ and diffusion coefficient σ, let b be the stochastic-exponential integrand and let Z be the process defined by source equation (7). Let the auxiliary diffusion have drift μ + bσ, coefficient σ, and initial point s₀. For every finite T > 0, Z is a martingale on [0,T] if and only if (the auxiliary diffusion does not exit at r or r is good) and (the auxiliary diffusion does not exit at l or l is good). Z is a nonnegative continuous local martingale under the setup, as stated on printed p. 5. Here exit means positive probability of a finite exit time with limit equal to the endpoint. Set ρ(x) = exp(−∫_c^x 2μ(y)/σ²(y) dy) and s(x) = ∫_c^x ρ(y) dy for c ∈ J. The endpoint r is good precisely when s(r) < ∞ and (s(r)−s)b²/(ρσ²) is integrable near r; l is good precisely when s(l) > −∞ and (s−s(l))b²/(ρσ²) is integrable near l. Endpoint goodness uses the original diffusion's scale. Under the additional assumption that S does not exit J, Corollary 2.2 states that Z is a martingale if and only if the auxiliary diffusion does not exit J.

Assumptions in the source: μ, σ, b are Borel functions on J; σ(x) ≠ 0 throughout J; 1/σ² and μ/σ² are locally integrable on J; b²/σ² is locally integrable on J. S is a weak solution on a filtered probability space carrying its driving Brownian motion. The filtration need not be generated by S or that Brownian motion. Both diffusions are absorbed at their exit endpoints. Up to exit time ζ, Z is the exponential of ∫ b(S)dW − (1/2)∫ b²(S)dt; after a finite exit with finite accumulated ∫ b²(S)dt it retains its exit value, and on a finite exit with infinite accumulated integral it is set to zero. These conventions are part of the input, not consequences to omit.

Simplifications we impose: Only rename source Y, P, x₀ as S, Q, s₀. Retain the full two-endpoint criterion; do not replace it by nonexit without checking endpoint goodness or the nonexit assumption of source Corollary 2.2. The future D1 application will set J = (0,∞), μ = 0 and b(x) = πσ(x)/x, but no specialization is asserted as proved here.

Formal: Structure `Upstream.MartingaleCriterion` in `lean/Upstream/MartingaleCriterion.lean`; shared vocabulary in `lean/Upstream/Diffusion.lean`. Fields: `nonneg_continuous_localMartingale` (p. 5), `martingale_iff` (Theorem 2.1, on [0,∞)), `martingaleOn_iff` (the Remark, every finite T > 0, with "martingale on [0,T]" encoded as `Martingale (stopAt T Z)`), `martingale_iff_of_noExit` (Corollary 2.2, premise: S exits at neither endpoint). Each field holds for every J = (l,r) with endpoints in EReal, Borel μ, σ with (5)–(6), Borel b with (8), s₀ ∈ J, every c ∈ J, and every (S, ζ, Z) that the opaque relations `SDEPrimitives.Solves` (weak solution of (4) with exit time, driven by an (𝓕,P)-Brownian motion W) and `SDEPrimitives.StochExp` (the exponential (7)) accept. Mathlib has no stochastic integral, so these two relations cannot be defined; `SDEPrimitives` ties each to the concrete conventions of p. 4, all almost sure and simultaneous in t: start at s₀, ζ > 0, S ∈ J and continuous before ζ, a finite ζ is an exit at l or r (limit in EReal), absorption after an exit at a finite endpoint, Z₀ = 1, Z > 0 before ζ, Z frozen at a positive value after a finite exit with finite ∫₀^ζ b²(S_u) du, and Z = 0 after one with infinite integral. After an exit at an infinite endpoint the real-valued S is unconstrained. Conditions (a)/(c) and the conclusion of Corollary 2.2 use the source's own "i.e. (20) is not satisfied" form: ṽ(r) = ∞, with ṽ = `fellerV (auxDrift μ σ b) σ c`. The lemma `scaleDensity_auxDrift` proves that its scale density is the source's ρ̃ of (17). Ỹ itself is not formalized. Goodness (`GoodRight`/`GoodLeft`) is (24)/(26) with the original scale. Instance: `MartingaleCriterion.vacuousInstance`, VACUOUS. It sits on a one-point base (Dirac mass, right-continuous trivial filtration, W ≡ 0), and every field is discharged by refuting its Brownian premise (`not_isFBrownian_unit`). The usual conditions do hold there. No degenerate non-vacuous model is within reach: Mathlib defines Brownian motion but does not construct one, and building it would reconstruct cited theory. Convention checks, rule 6: in the same file `mu_trivial_check` (μ = 0, σ = 1, b = 0 on ℝ, Z ≡ 1: every conclusion holds on both sides), `mu_killed_check` (a killed Z is not a martingale, so the martingale side can fail), `goodRight_zero_check` and `not_goodRight_ramp_check` ((24) true for b = 0, false for b = 1/(1−x) at r = 1); the shared checks are listed in the header of `Diffusion.lean`. Not shown: a case where the MUCondition side is false (that needs ṽ in closed form for a non-trivial b). Every theorem using this structure is conditional on the two primitives being the sources' weak-solution relation and stochastic exponential. The structure/instance audit is outstanding.

### AX-04  Bounded stopping of a continuous local martingale
Source: [hulley2019weak], Section 2, opening paragraph and Lemma 2.1, printed p. 3; notation (1.1), printed p. 1, and Condition (B), printed p. 2; `refs/text/hulley2019weak.md`. This is the stored working-paper version, dated October 9, 2018 on its first page.

Statement (source notation):

> Lemma 2.1. Suppose M ∈ Mloc satisfies Condition (B). Then M τλ ∈ M, for all λ ≥ 0.

Statement (our notation): Let S be a real-valued continuous local martingale under Q on a right-continuous filtration, with deterministic S₀ = s₀ > 0. For every n > s₀ let τₙ = inf{t ≥ 0 : |S_t| > n}, with inf ∅ = ∞, and Sⁿ_t = S_(t∧τₙ). Then Sⁿ is a uniformly integrable martingale. It is bounded in absolute value by n. If S is nonnegative, so is Sⁿ. The boundedness follows directly from continuity, including the value at τₙ; no overshoot convention is being imposed on discontinuous processes.

Assumptions in the source: A right-continuous filtration; adapted, real-valued càdlàg local martingales with integrable initial value. Mloc denotes these local martingales and M denotes uniformly integrable martingales. The source defines τλ = inf{t ≥ 0 : |M_t| > λ}. Condition (B) is lim_(λ→∞) E[1_{τλ<∞}|ΔM_τλ|] = 0, where ΔM = M − M₋. For a continuous process every jump is zero, so (B) holds identically, without an integrability estimate. The Section 2 opening paragraph explicitly records the continuous bounded-stopping case.

Simplifications we impose: Restrict to continuous paths, deterministic strictly positive initial value, and n > s₀. For the D8 application also restrict to nonnegative S. These are subclasses of the source's hypotheses; we do not reconstruct the stopping theorem or its stochastic proof. In particular a frozen process is allowed: this input does not assert that Sⁿ remains a diffusion with nowhere-zero coefficient on (0,∞).

Formal: Structure `Upstream.BoundedStopping` in `lean/Upstream/BoundedStopping.lean`. Field `stopped_martingale`: on a base (Ω, Q, 𝓕), for every S satisfying `Stopping.ContLocMart` (Q a probability, 𝓕 right-continuous, S adapted, all paths continuous, S₀ = s₀ > 0 deterministic, S a local martingale in Mathlib's `Locally` sense) and every n > s₀, the process stopped at `Stopping.exitTime` τₙ = inf{t : |S_t| > n} (inf ∅ = ∞) is a martingale. The deterministic parts of the statement are proved, not assumed: |Sⁿ| ≤ n (`abs_stopAtExit_le`, from path continuity), nonnegativity (`stopAtExit_nonneg`), and uniform integrability (`uniformIntegrable_stopAtExit`). Instance: `BoundedStopping.degenerateInstance`, DEGENERATE and genuine, not vacuous. It is on the one-point base, where every local martingale is constant in time (`unit_localMartingale_const`), so the field holds for every admissible S there. The premises are satisfiable (`constant_model`), and τₙ = ∞ for the constant process with n > s₀ (`exitTime_const`). It is no evidence for non-constant processes. Frozen processes are allowed. The structure/instance audit is outstanding.

### AX-05  Sin's stochastic-volatility model has both kinds of pricing measure
Source: [sin1995complications], Theorem 3.2, printed p. 4 (PDF p. 6), Theorem 3.6 and its preceding nonparallel-vectors qualification, printed p. 5 (PDF p. 7); Section 2 and Assumptions 2.3, 3.1, printed pp. 2–4; `refs/text/sin1995complications.md`. Scanned Cornell report 1139, not the still-wanted journal version [sin1998complications]. Equations checked visually against the PDF on 2026-10-06 by math; source audit is still required.

Statement (source notation):

> Then S_t is a martingale under Q if and only if (a · σ) ≤ 0.

This is Theorem 3.2's conclusion. Theorem 3.6's conclusion includes:

> S_t is a martingale on [0,T].

Theorem 3.6 supplies the equivalent measure for each finite horizon; its equations and all qualifications are retained in our notation below rather than quoting the entire theorem.

Statement (our notation): Use Sin's equations dS_t = S_t v_t^α γ · dW_t, S₀ = s₀ > 0, and dv_t = v_t a · dW_t + κ(L−v_t)dt, v₀ = 1, under a probability Qˢ on a usual filtered space carrying a two-dimensional Brownian motion W. Here α, κ, L > 0 are constants and γ,a ∈ ℝ² are constant nonzero vectors. Rename the source σ as γ to avoid confusion with the local-volatility coefficient, and its ρ as κ to avoid confusion with scale density. These equations have a finite, continuous, strictly positive solution as in the source setup and Remark 2.2/Proposition 4.1; S is a nonnegative continuous local martingale. Theorem 3.2 says S is a martingale on all finite horizons iff a · γ ≤ 0. Thus a · γ > 0 implies it fails the martingale property on at least one finite horizon; we do not strengthen this to every finite horizon from the theorem's wording alone. If also |a · γ| < |a||γ| (nonparallel vectors), Theorem 3.6 supplies, for each T > 0, Qᵐ_T equivalent to Qˢ on F_T under which S is a martingale on [0,T]. For a · γ > 0 it gives dynamics dv_t = v_t a · dWᵐ_t + (κL − (a · γ)v_t^(1+α))dt, with the same price equation and initial values. The horizon-specific measures need not be asserted equivalent on an infinite-horizon sigma field.

Assumptions in the source: Section 2 imposes α > 0, positive bounded mean-reversion parameters, deterministic bounded nonzero diffusion vectors, usual filtration conditions and trivial initial sigma field. Assumption 3.1 specializes to constant coefficients and zero interest. Nonparallel diffusion vectors are essential to Theorem 3.6 and are stated immediately before it, even though not repeated inside its displayed statement. The preliminary Assumption 2.3 concerns reaching Q from the physical-measure equations using a true exponential density. Our use starts directly under Qˢ with zero price drift, so that preliminary change has density one. No claim is made for parallel vectors.

Simplifications we impose: For D8 choose α = κ = L = 1, γ = (1,0), a = (1,1). Their dot product is 1 > 0 and 1 < √2 = |a||γ|. The source's coefficient bounds hold with any constant exceeding √2. These choices satisfy all the named restrictions and exhibit strictness under Qˢ and an equivalent true-martingale measure on any chosen finite horizon; no Girsanov or explosion proof is reconstructed. The D8 observation will keep its physical measure fixed while varying which of these measures is the pricing measure.

Formal: Structure `Upstream.SinPricingMeasures` in `lean/Upstream/SinPricingMeasures.lean`. Solving Sin's system cannot be defined without stochastic integration, so it is the opaque relation `Sin.SinPrimitives.Solves`, tied to the concrete conventions `SinConventions`: adapted, and on every path continuous, S₀ = s₀, v₀ = 1 and S > 0 (a version the source's construction allows). Euclidean quantities are written out: `dot`, and `Nonparallel` as (a·γ)² < |a|²|γ|². The premises (`Sin.Setting`) are a two-dimensional (𝓕,Q)-Brownian motion (each component (𝓕,Q)-Brownian, the two independent, joint increments independent of 𝓕_s), the usual conditions, a trivial 𝓕₀, and `SinParams.Valid` (α, κ, L > 0, γ, a ≠ 0, s₀ > 0). Fields: `localMartingale`; `martingale_iff` (Theorem 3.2: martingale iff a·γ ≤ 0); and `equivalent_martingale_measure` (Theorem 3.6: for a·γ > 0 and nonparallel vectors, every T > 0 has a probability Qᵐ equivalent to Q on 𝓕_T under which S is a martingale on [0,T]). The Qᵐ-dynamics of v are not a field. `Sin.exists_strict_horizon` derives "some finite T₀ with S not a martingale on [0,T₀]" from Theorem 3.2, and is not strengthened to every horizon. Instance: `SinPricingMeasures.vacuousInstance`, VACUOUS, on the one-point base with W₁ = W₂ ≡ 0, refuting the Brownian premise, for the same reason as AX-01; a constant process could not witness strictness in any case. Convention checks in the same file: `sin_parameter_check` (D8's α = κ = L = 1, γ = (1,0), a = (1,1) is valid, with a·γ = 1 and nonparallel), `sin_conventions_check` (constant paths satisfy the conventions; `EquivOn` is reflexive) and `sin_conclusion_check` (martingale, non-martingale and martingale-on-[0,T] are all inhabited). The structure/instance audit is outstanding.

### AX-06  Nonnegative local martingales are supermartingales
Source: [hulley2019weak], Section 2, paragraph immediately following the proof of Lemma 2.1, printed p. 3; `refs/text/hulley2019weak.md`. Also [mijatovic2010martingale], Discussion immediately after (9), printed p. 5; `refs/text/mijatovic2010martingale.md`, explicitly uses the same general implication for its nonnegative continuous local martingale Z without any failed-jump-condition qualification.

Statement (source notation):

> Since M is then a non-negative supermartingale

The sentence is in the paragraph beginning with a nonnegative M ∈ Mloc; the quoted clause records the textbook property being used, not a new conclusion of that paper.

Statement (our notation): An adapted, real-valued, nonnegative local martingale S with E_Q[S₀] < ∞ on a right-continuous filtration is a supermartingale: each S_t is integrable and E_Q[S_t | F_u] ≤ S_u almost surely for 0 ≤ u ≤ t. In particular E_Q[S_t] ≤ E_Q[S_u].

Assumptions in the source: The Section 1 setup of real-valued càdlàg local martingales with integrable initial value on a right-continuous filtration; nonnegativity.

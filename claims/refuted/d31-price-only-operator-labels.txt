Refuted within the declared scalar observation contract: call/put price
vectors alone always identify one of expectation pricing, parity-restoring
pricing, or an American-call benchmark, given only AX-18 and claim 017's
American lower-bound restriction.

Normalize spot to one, take terminal mean m=3/5 and defect delta=2/5.
A finite terminal-payoff fixture gives equal mass to 0 and 6/5. At strikes
1/4, 3/4, 3/2 the expectation calls are (19/40, 9/40, 0) and the puts
are (1/8, 3/8, 9/10). Restored calls add delta, giving
(7/8, 5/8, 2/5). The declared American equality benchmark A_Q=c+delta
gives exactly the same vector, and satisfies the supplied lower bound.
Both residual vectors are zero despite positive delta. A common quote
observation mechanism acting on these vectors gives the same law under
both labels, so no test on that price subvector distinguishes them.

This is scalar compatibility, not a proof that the finite terminal fixture
extends to a local-martingale process or that its American stopping value
equals that benchmark. Claim 017 does not assert attainability of every
scalar value satisfying its bound. Contract metadata can distinguish
exercise styles; equal price vectors do not hide those metadata.

Strongest corrected design: specify the contracts and valuation rules,
report compatible conventions, and test European zero residual using
supplied simultaneous price coverage. The expectation/restored residual
gap is delta, with a guaranteed strict separation at delta>2h under
quote-error bound h. Restored-versus-American separation fails on the
equality face unless an additional observable and its own pricing law
break the overlap. See checks/d31-option-design/README.md and check.py.

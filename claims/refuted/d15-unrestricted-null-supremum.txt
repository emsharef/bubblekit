Mechanism without model nouns: a positive-weight narrow component can fit one
observation exactly, while other positive-weight components support the rest.
This is the known Gaussian-mixture singularity [snoussi2001mixtures], Proposition
1, not a new general theorem. The constrained parameter paths below are the
D15 application checks. AX-59's stochastic source audit is pending; these are
calculations for the explicitly defined density functions, not an approved
statistical claim or a proof of a pricing-null model.

Poisson mixture. Fix any n>=1 finite returns R_j and positive durations Δ_j.
Let h_J be the function displayed in AX-59; fix λ_J>0, η_J>0 and finite μ_J.
Choose a_J=R_1/Δ_1 and let 0<σ_J<=1 tend to zero. In the first factor keep
only count zero; in every other factor keep only count one. Positivity gives

  product_j h_J(R_j;Δ_j) >= exp(-λ_J sum_j Δ_j)
     /[sqrt(2πΔ_1) σ_J]
     * product_(j>1) [λ_JΔ_j φ(R_j; a_JΔ_j+μ_J, σ_J²Δ_j+η_J²)].

Each latter Gaussian factor is bounded below uniformly in 0<σ_J<=1 by

  exp(-(R_j-a_JΔ_j-μ_J)²/(2η_J²)) / sqrt(2π(η_J²+Δ_j)) > 0.

Thus the displayed product is at least a strictly positive constant divided
by σ_J. The supremum is infinite at EVERY finite return vector, even with
jump intensity, mean and positive variance fixed. One does not need large
intensity, a jump at every increment, or shrinking jump variance. Multiplying
by the fixed positive price Jacobian product_j(1/y_j) changes none of this.
The zero-at-infinite-denominator convention would give zero evidence on all
positive arrays for this unrestricted envelope. A numerical parameter grid
that reports a finite maximum has simply missed the singularity.

Two-regime volatility, even with common physical drift. For n>=2 allow a
change after the first increment. Give the first segment scale σ_J and the
second a fixed κ>0. Set b=R_1/Δ_1+σ_J²/2. The first Gaussian log-return mean
is exactly R_1, so its density is 1/[sqrt(2πΔ_1) σ_J]. The remaining factors
have mean (b-κ²/2)Δ_j and variance κ²Δ_j; each tends to a finite strictly
positive value as σ_J tends to zero. Their product times σ_J therefore has
a positive limit. The joint supremum is infinite at every finite return
vector. Allowing separate segment drifts cannot repair this. This example
uses one switch, not independent fitted regimes at each time.

Restrictions that leave a route open (not a proved power claim).
1. A fixed σ_J>=σ_min>0 bounds every component of h_J by
   1/sqrt(2π σ_min² Δ). Since its weights sum to one, this also bounds h_J,
   uniformly in log drift, intensity and jump parameters. The product times
   the price Jacobian is a finite upper envelope. For a fixed parameter and
   count cutoff K_J, the omitted density is bounded by that same constant
   times P(Poisson(λ_JΔ)>K_J). A truncated sum alone is a LOWER bound and
   cannot certify a composite-null denominator. Uniform bounds over a search
   domain, and its optimization error, remain necessary for a tighter score.
   No source audit, lower scale justification or useful power is supplied by
   these algebraic bounds. Penalized likelihood is also not a replacement
   for an unpenalized pointwise dominating denominator.
2. For one switch, fix permitted cut indices so each segment has at least two
   increments. Enlarge further to separate drift and scale per segment.
   Claim 055's already-proved Gaussian profile computes each segment's exact
   supremum; multiply the two and maximize over the finite permitted cuts.
   This dominates the common-drift one-switch family. It is finite whenever
   every permitted segment has positive weighted residual Q_G. The exceptional
   zero-residual segments still give infinity; they cannot silently be removed
   from the observation domain. With at least two increments each exceptional
   set has Lebesgue measure zero, but rounded observations need not avoid it.
   This does not cover arbitrary repeated switches, a hidden-state model with
   singleton runs, or general stochastic volatility.
